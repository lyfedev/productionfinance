Methodology

This page explains how every figure on this site is computed. It does not depend on any particular comparison, and its address stays the same — a link to it can be sent on its own.

The pipeline, stage by stage

Every candidate city is priced through the same fixed sequence, applied identically regardless of which jurisdiction a city sits in:

  1. Canonical budget. One production budget is built from the description a visitor typed in, before any city enters the calculation — the same budget is then priced against every candidate city, so no city gets a differently-shaped production to compare against.
  2. Localization. That one budget is priced against one city's committed cost profile: labour, fringe, housing, per diem, flights, stages, equipment, permits, locations and trucking, each read from that city's own dated rate data.
  3. Qualifying base. For a city whose jurisdiction has a committed incentive rule file, the spend that programme's rules actually count is computed from the rule file's own declared definition — never a flat percentage assumed the same everywhere.
  4. Credit calculation. A fixed, ordered sequence of adjustments — per-person ceiling, uplift stacking, the tier or blend rate, the per-project cap, the annual programme cap — applied in exactly that order, every time, with every step disclosed even when it changes nothing.
  5. Net cash conversion. The gross credit is converted to what actually arrives as cash: the audit fee is deducted, the programme's own mechanism (rebate, transferable credit, refundable credit) is applied, and the estimated timing of arrival is recorded alongside the amount.
  6. Landed cost aggregation. The city's cost lines are summed into a cost total; when a net-cash incentive was priced, it is subtracted to produce the total landed cost. When no incentive is modelled, the total landed cost equals the cost total exactly — it is never treated as though the missing incentive were worth $0.
  7. Ranking. Every city that produced a real net-cash total is placed in one ranked band, sorted by total landed cost. Every other city — a real cost total, but no modelled incentive — sits in a separate band and is never sorted alongside the first, since the two totals are not the same kind of number.
  8. Gap decomposition. For the two leading cities, the difference between their totals is broken into the same components the totals were built from, so a headline gap is never a single unexplained number.

Where a number came from: the basis axis

Every cost-side figure carries a basis naming where its value came from. It is shown on every figure on the assumptions panel, next to — never merged with — that figure's confidence tier.

sourced
A published rate-card row: a union scale, a per-diem table, a stage rate card — read directly from a dated, cited document.
estimated
Computed from a sourced input by a disclosed method — for example, a fringe multiplier applied to a published wage scale, or a regional index. The method is stated in the figure's own derivation.
modelling_assumption
No public source exists for this number anywhere — crew composition and department spend ratios are the primary example. Disclosed as exactly that, never dressed up as a sourced figure.

A total inherits the WEAKEST basis among everything that fed into it. A total built from one modelling_assumption line is itself labelled modelling_assumption — it never reports a stronger tier than its weakest input actually earned.

Two different questions, never one badge

Two separate labels appear on every figure, answering two separate questions:

basis (sourced / estimated / modelling_assumption)
Where did this number come from?
confidence (validated / researched)
Has this specific computed figure been checked against a real government disclosure, or is it only research-backed?

A third, unrelated vocabulary exists elsewhere on this site for a different subject entirely: a cited source DOCUMENT's own reliability tier (a four-level scale from low to high). That tier describes how reliable a document is; it is never the same field as a figure's own confidence, and the two are never combined into a single label.

The honest-refusal states

Three situations get an explicit, worded state rather than a guessed number:

  • A named but unpriced city. A candidate city typed in that resolves to no committed cost profile at all is named on the page exactly as typed — never silently dropped from the list.
  • An unmodelled incentive. A city with a real cost total but no committed, modelled incentive shows "unknown — not modelled," never a $0 incentive. A missing number and a genuinely zero number are different facts, and this site does not conflate them.
  • A fixed total budget. This site does not accept a single fixed dollar amount as an input, because cost is only ever an output here — a fixed dollar figure buys a different production in every city, which makes a same-budget comparison circular rather than informative.
