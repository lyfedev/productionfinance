<assumption_delta_decision>
**The generalization:** Phase 2 introduces national and regional programme stacking. A
single jurisdiction can carry more than one incentive programme, and success criterion 2
requires their stacking order to be modelled.

**Primary noun:** `Programme`. Jurisdiction becomes an attribute a programme carries, not
the entity being priced.

**Decision: `promote`.**

**Rationale:** every computed quantity in this engine hangs off a programme, never off a
jurisdiction — `base_definition`, `rate_structure`, `per_person_ceiling`, `caps`,
`mechanism`, `audit`, `timing` and `validation` are all programme fields. A jurisdiction
contributes only container attributes: currency, level, `parent_id` and the citation set.
Promoting `Programme` to the engine's iteration unit means a national-plus-regional stack
is N programmes priced independently and summed in dollars, with N as a genuine variable —
rather than one programme plus a second one handled as a special case. Plan 02-01 already
wrote `price_jurisdiction` as a loop over `programmes` for exactly this reason, so the
promotion is being *made explicit* here, not introduced late.

**Storage note:** the YAML file stays one-per-jurisdiction, as ARCHITECTURE.md Q2 has it.
That is a curation and audit convenience — a reviewer opens one file and sees everything a
jurisdiction offers — not the identity model. The two are allowed to differ, and this note
exists so a later reader does not infer the identity from the filename.

**Invariant test encoding the generalized intent:**
`tests/test_engine_credit.py::test_stacking_prices_every_declared_programme` — a fixture
jurisdiction declaring N stackable programmes produces N independently-computed dollar
figures summed at the top, asserted for N equal to 1, 2 and 3 through the identical code
path. A first-programme-only bug passes at N equal to 1 and fails at 2 and 3.

**Open, and deliberately not decided here:** whether a *regional* programme belongs in its
parent jurisdiction's file as a second `programmes` entry, or in its own file linked by
`parent_id`. ARCHITECTURE.md Q2 offers both mechanisms and picks neither. That question is
the checkpoint below.
</assumption_delta_decision>

Implement steps four and five of the credit sequence and the availability determination.

The per-project cap clips. It is a rule about what this project may be entitled to, so it
reduces the credit value: `min(credit, cap)`, with a derivation line naming the cap and
whether it bound. The comparison is strictly greater-than, so a credit exactly at the cap
is not clipped — the three-point boundary test proves it. A programme declaring no
per-project cap emits a line saying so.

The annual programme cap does **not** clip. RD-04 in `02-01-PLAN.md` records the reasoning:
INC-05 requires availability to be answered separately from eligibility, and clipping the
credit by the programme's remaining allocation would fuse the two answers into one number
that means neither. Step five emits a derivation line recording the declared cap, its
period and the declared consumption-check method, feeds the remaining-allocation figure
into the availability determination, and leaves the credit value untouched. Add a comment
at this call site recording `02-RESEARCH.md` Open Question 1 — whether a future
jurisdiction needs caps applied per sub-component before summing rather than as one global
clip — so a later reinterpretation is a visible change.

`assess_availability` produces a three-state answer. Given a remaining-allocation figure at
or above the computed credit, `available` is true. Below it, `available` is false with a
reason naming both figures. Given no figure at all, `available` is `None` with a reason
stating that consumption state was not fetched in this run — Phase 7's freshness gate owns
fetching it, and defaulting an unknown to true would be the single most misleading
simplification available in this whole engine. The Czech Republic case in
`feasibility-incentives.md` is the reason this matters: a programme whose terms were
entirely stable while its money ran out mid-year is unrepresentable if availability is
inferred from the rules.

Eligibility is its own field with its own reasons: minimum spend met or not, programme
status open or not, mutual exclusivity resolved. An ineligible production still gets an
availability answer — the two are computed independently, and suppressing one because the
other failed is exactly the conflation INC-05 forbids.

Record in `jurisdictions/SCOPE-FREEZE.md` that partial allocation is deliberately not
modelled: where the remaining allocation is smaller than the credit, this phase reports
unavailable rather than splitting the award across periods. Name it as a disclosed
simplification with the phase that would own it, not as a gap.
