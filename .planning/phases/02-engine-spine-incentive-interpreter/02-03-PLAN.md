<objective>
Widen the qualifying-base stage from the one type the tracer proved to all four
jurisdictions actually use, plus the escape hatch — and make the minimum-spend cliff a
tested step function rather than an assumption.

This is the stage where jurisdictions genuinely disagree about what money even counts.
Total local spend, labour only, the lesser of a percentage of core spend or actual local
spend, and local hires only are four different answers from one identical budget. Getting
this wrong produces a number that looks right and is wrong by tens of percent.

Purpose: INC-01 and INC-09. Output: the complete base-definition dispatch, a closed
handler registry that is actually exercised, and boundary tests at every cliff.
</objective>

  <action>
Implement the minimum-spend step in `engine/qualifying_base.py` as a hard step function
evaluated after the base-definition dispatch has produced a base. Below the declared
threshold the base becomes exactly zero. At or above it, the base is unchanged. There is
no proportional region, no taper and no interpolation — a production one dollar below a
threshold gets a categorically different answer from one a dollar above it, and that is
the actual behaviour of every jurisdiction that declares one.

Both branches append a derivation line. The below-threshold line names the declared
threshold and the base that fell below it. The at-or-above line states the threshold was
met. A programme declaring no threshold emits a line stating none is declared. Three
distinct lines, never silence.

Order matters and must be recorded in a comment at the call site: the threshold is
compared against the *qualifying base*, not against the raw total spend, because a
jurisdiction whose base definition is labour-only asks whether the qualifying labour
cleared the bar, not whether the production's total budget did. This is the same class of
ordering hazard as applying a per-person ceiling to the output instead of the base.
