  <decision>Fix the validation boundary: what a validation pair is allowed to prove</decision>
  <context>
    CONTEXT.md D-02 draws the line everything downstream is built on, and rates it one-way. It states that a validation pair proves the incentive interpreter only, never cost localization: government disclosures publish qualified spend and the award, but not the production's input vector. `feasibility-incentives.md` line 263 says so explicitly — the disclosures give total qualified spend and top-line labour, hours and wages, not the full input vector. So a fixture feeds qualified spend IN as a given and asserts only on net cash OUT. The inputs-to-qualified-spend half of the pipeline has no government ground truth and must never carry a validated label.

    Why this is one-way rather than a schema choice: this boundary is the honesty claim of the entire product. Phase 8's proof panel (PRV-06) publishes an accuracy figure on the hosted page, and every validated-versus-researched badge in the Phase 6 interface (PRV-02) inherits its meaning from here. Blurring the line later means retracting a public accuracy figure, not refactoring a test.

    What accepting this costs: the headline claim becomes that the incentive interpreter reproduces published government awards exactly, rather than the broader claim that the model reproduces government awards. The cost model — the larger and more visible half of the product — is never validated against anything, and the product must say so on screen.

    This checkpoint exists because CONTEXT.md flagged D-02 one-way and because it was Claude's judgment under a general delegation rather than a choice you made explicitly. Confirming it takes one word; discovering in Phase 8 that it was wrong does not.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="interpreter-only">
      <name>Interpreter-only boundary (recommended — implements D-02 as written)</name>
      <pros>Every validated claim the product makes is literally true and independently checkable against a government PDF. The accuracy figure means one specific, defensible thing. Fixtures stay small and Phase 5's mismatch taxonomy has a clean denominator.</pros>
      <cons>The cost-localization half ships with no validation at all and must be labelled as such everywhere it appears. A judge could read the accuracy figure as covering more than it does unless the labelling is careful.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="full-pipeline">
      <name>Attempt full-pipeline validation, reconstructing input vectors</name>
      <pros>A single headline accuracy number covering inputs through to net cash would be a stronger claim if it held.</pros>
      <cons>The input vector is not disclosed anywhere, so it would have to be reverse-engineered from the answer — which makes the test circular and the accuracy figure meaningless. This is the specific failure PROJECT.md's honesty constraint forbids.</cons>
    </option>

      <name>Interpreter-only now, with a separately-labelled second tier added later</name>
      <pros>Keeps the D-02 boundary intact today while leaving room for a cost-model sanity tier in Milestone 2 that is explicitly not called validation.</pros>
      <cons>Adds a schema dimension nothing in Milestone 1 consumes, and the second tier has no government ground truth either — a plausibility check wearing validation's clothes. Better deferred than designed now.</cons>
    </option>

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| esd.ny.gov → local archive | Untrusted bytes fetched over the network become the project's cited source of truth |
| archived document → committed fixture | A transcription step where a wrong or invented digit becomes an authoritative-looking figure |
| repository → public reader | Every archived document and fixture is published and inspectable |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-01-12 | Tampering | archived government documents under `sources/` | high | mitigate | Every archived document's sha256 is computed at retrieval and recorded in `sources/MANIFEST.yaml`; each fixture cites both the path and that sha256, so a substituted document is detectable by re-hashing. Task 2's automated verify re-hashes the file and compares. |
| T-01-13 | Spoofing | source URL authenticity | medium | mitigate | Only the official `esd.ny.gov` document URL is recorded, fetched over HTTPS, and the effective final URL after redirects is what the manifest records — not the URL that was requested. |
| T-01-14 | Tampering | figure transcription from a summarising tool | high | mitigate | Figures are extracted with `pdftotext -layout` over the archived bytes, never from an LLM summary of the PDF and never copied from `feasibility-incentives.md`; the extracted values are cross-checked against the independently-recorded research values and any discrepancy is reported rather than reconciled. Recorded as a prohibition in `must_haves.prohibitions`. |
| T-01-15 | Spoofing | a fixture set that asserts nothing | high | mitigate | The suite fails collection on an empty glob, asserts non-empty required fields, and asserts filename uniqueness; the acceptance criteria require observing the hard failure with the fixture directory moved aside. |
| T-01-16 | Information Disclosure | archived documents published in a public repository | low | accept | These are already-public government disclosures whose republication with a hash is the entire point of the audit trail. No personal data beyond what the state published. |
| T-01-SC | Tampering | package-manager installs | high | mitigate | No new installs in this plan. `pyyaml` and `pytest` were resolved in plan 01-01 under the audit table in 01-RESEARCH.md § "Package Legitimacy Audit" — all Approved, no `[SLOP]`, no genuine risk signal — and `lockfile-scan` guards the resolved set on every commit. |
</threat_model>
