# Pitfalls Research: ProductionFinance

**Domain:** Film/TV production incentive modelling + live government-data research agent, built for a partner-track hackathon with a hard 17-day deadline
**Researched:** 2026-08-23
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (domain pitfalls verified against primary/vendor sources this session; delivery pitfalls verified against hackathon-brief.md and general Devpost/GitHub mechanics; a few items — Georgia withholding rate, CA clawback mechanics — remain probabilistic, flagged inline)

**Relationship to existing research:** `feasibility-incentives.md` and `idea-2-incentives.md` already name and correctly resolve: naive-rate-on-total-budget, per-person ceilings (GA $500K W-2 vs loan-out), the audit fee, the ~85–92¢ transfer discount, UK gross-vs-net taxable credit, eligibility-vs-availability, the NY $700M/$800M figure conflict (unresolved in the source docs), the GA loan-out withholding ambiguity (4.99%/5.75%, unresolved), NM HB 237, base-isn't-total-spend generally, mutually-exclusive uplifts, residency splits in Ontario, and the Wrapbook hallucination-disclosure competitive point. **This document does not repeat those as findings** — it builds on them, resolves two of the open items where new research permits, and adds pitfalls the supplied briefs do not cover. Every pitfall below is marked **[NEW]** unless it is explicitly extending a named-but-unresolved open item, marked **[EXTENDS]**.

## TOP FIVE — RANKED, PROJECT-SINKING

These are the failures that plausibly end the project, not just cost points. Ranked by (likelihood × blast radius) inside a 17-day window.

### #1 — A validated jurisdiction produces a number that does not match the government's, and nobody notices before the demo
**Why it sinks the project:** The entire pitch is "we reproduce a government-published figure exactly." One visibly wrong number in the four curated jurisdictions, surfaced live or by a technically literate judge poking at the hosted URL, doesn't just cost Technological Implementation points — it inverts the core claim into evidence of overclaiming, which is explicitly what the project says it refuses to do (Constraints: "Never present a researched figure as validated"). See **Critical Pitfall 1 and 2** below.

### #2 — The Google Cloud SDK is imported but never actually exercised at runtime, or Parallel Search is stubbed for the demo
**Why it sinks the project:** Both are Stage One pass/fail gates, and Stage One is *partly automated* — meaning some part of this check may run without a human reading your justification. An unused import, a code path that only calls Parallel/Gemini in a dev script never invoked by the shipped app, or a demo that fakes Job 2's live-research call to avoid latency risk, is a disqualification, not a low score. See **Critical Pitfall 3**.

### #3 — Normalization scope expands without bound and nothing ships
**Why it sinks the project:** The source research itself calls normalization "the hard part... not research or arithmetic" and every jurisdiction has multiple axes of irreducible complexity (base definition, uplifts, caps, residency, gross-to-net). With 17 days and four curated jurisdictions plus an open-ended Job 2, the single highest-probability failure mode on a project of this shape is a team still refining Georgia's per-person cap logic on day 15 with no working UI. See **Scope Trap 1**.

### #4 — The hosted URL fails for the judge, who is by definition an anonymous, cold, unauthenticated visitor
**Why it sinks the project:** "Hosted URL is required and must work for an anonymous visitor" is explicit in both the brief and the PROJECT.md Definition of Done. A cold-start timeout on first load, an auth wall left in from development, a CORS failure between a separately-hosted frontend and backend, or a region mismatch that makes the demo fast for the builder and slow/broken for everyone else, fails Definition of Done #1 outright. See **Delivery Pitfall 3**.

### #5 — The live-research demo (Job 2) hangs, errors, or returns garbage in front of the judges, live
**Why it sinks the project:** Job 2 is a named Definition of Done item and one of the four required demo beats. It is also the single most failure-prone part of the system — it depends on three external services (Parallel Search, Gemini extraction, and whatever government page happens to be live and well-formed that day) chained together with no curated fallback. A single flaky run recorded into a fixed demo video is a survivable one-time risk; relying on it to work *live* for judges, or having no recorded fallback if the hosted demo flakes, is not. See **Scope Trap 3** and **Delivery Pitfall 5**.

## PART A — DOMAIN / MODELLING PITFALLS

### A1. Incentive arithmetic traps beyond what the briefs already name

#### A1.1 — Stacking: national + regional programmes compounding on the wrong base **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** Two incentive layers (e.g., Canada's OPSTC 21.5% + federal PSTC 16%, or a US state programme layered under a city/county add-on) look additive but are computed on *different, overlapping bases* — one on total qualifying expenditure, one on labour only, one net of the other's assistance. Naively summing the two percentages, or applying both to the same base figure, overstates the combined value.
**Why it happens:** The mental model of "stack the incentives" is a spreadsheet habit (add two percentages) that doesn't survive contact with two programmes defining "qualifying spend" differently. The feasibility research documents this precisely for Ontario/Canada federal (OPSTC + PSTC "combine — but on different, overlapping bases") but the project's own model needs to generalize this to any national+regional pair it encounters via Job 2, not just the one worked example.
**Prevention:** Model each jurisdiction's programme as its own base-definition object; never sum percentages, always sum *dollar outputs* computed independently against each programme's own base, then check explicitly for a "grinding" clause (one programme's assistance reduces the other's qualifying base — the Canadian CRA "assistance" rule is the canonical example) before summing.
**Warning sign:** Any code path that stores "combined rate" as a single number rather than deriving it from two independent base×rate computations.
**Milestone:** Accounts — normalization phase, before any jurisdiction with a national+regional split (Canada, if reached via Job 2) is trusted.

#### A1.2 — Above-the-line vs below-the-line spend treated as fungible **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** Several jurisdictions treat ATL (producer, director, writer, principal cast) compensation differently from BTL (crew) spend — separate caps, separate eligibility, or exclusion entirely. NY removed its $500K ATL comp cap in the 2025 budget change (documented in feasibility-incentives.md §3) — meaning a model built against a stale primer that still caps ATL comp in NY will systematically *under*-value NY relative to its current statute. The general risk is broader than the one NY case: any jurisdiction may split ATL/BTL treatment, and a model that treats "cast" and "crew" spend as one undifferentiated labour line cannot represent that split at all.
**Prevention:** The budget schema (six physical inputs → landed cost) must carry an ATL/BTL split as a first-class field, not just a "principal cast count," so that a jurisdiction rule referencing ATL compensation specifically has something to bind to. Test: can the model correctly answer "what changes if the lead actor is paid $500K more, all else equal" for NY pre- and post-2025 rules? If not, the schema is too coarse.
**Milestone:** Accounts — input schema design, before jurisdiction rule-encoding begins.

#### A1.3 — Residency and "local hire" definitions asserted, not proved **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** "Local hires only" (named in idea-2-incentives.md as one of several base definitions) sounds like a simple filter, but every jurisdiction defines residency differently and inconsistently with its neighbours: Ontario requires Ontario tax residency as of Dec 31 before principal photography; the federal Canadian credit requires Canadian residency; a crew member from British Columbia working an Ontario shoot counts federally but not provincially. The system's six inputs include "crew imported vs. hired locally" as a *user-declared* fact — but the user's own definition of "local" may not match the jurisdiction's legal test, and the model has no way to catch the mismatch.
**Prevention:** Do not let "hired locally" be a single boolean threaded unchanged into every jurisdiction's formula. Store it as a fact about the crew member (which state/province do they file resident tax in) and let each jurisdiction's own residency test consume that fact independently. Add an explicit caveat in the UI wherever a jurisdiction's residency test is stricter than "same city" (e.g., "counts as non-resident in Ontario provincial credit despite being Canadian").
**Milestone:** Accounts — jurisdiction rule-encoding phase.

#### A1.4 — Qualified vs non-qualified post-production/VFX spend modelled as ordinary production spend **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** Post/VFX spend frequently has its own threshold, its own rate, and its own eligibility window, separate from principal photography spend — NY cut its post/VFX qualifying threshold from "20% of VFX budget or $3M" to "10% or $500K" in 2025; the UK's VFX uplift (5%, net 29.25%) applies only within standard AVEC and is explicitly *not available to IFTC claimants*, and UK VFX costs are exempted from the general 80%-of-core cap that otherwise governs everything else. A model that pours all spend (production + post) into one undifferentiated pool will either miss an uplift the production is entitled to, or wrongly apply an uplift meant only for VFX to the whole budget.
**Prevention:** Post-production/VFX spend needs to be a separately taggable line in the budget model from day one, even if Milestone 1 doesn't model post-heavy productions in depth — because at least one curated jurisdiction (NY) has rules that reference it explicitly, and getting NY wrong is un-survivable (see Critical Pitfall 1).
**Milestone:** Accounts — input schema and NY rule-encoding.

#### A1.5 — The qualifying base computed net of the incentive itself (circularity) **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** In some jurisdictions the received incentive dollar amount is itself taxable income (UK is the documented case — 34%/53% gross, taxed as company income), which means the "true" landed cost calculation needs the credit amount to compute the tax on the credit, but the credit amount is partly a function of the base, which is a function of spend, which in a fully-loaded model *could* include costs that are themselves adjusted by tax treatment. This is a lower-probability trap for the four curated US jurisdictions (none currently tax the credit as company income at the state level for a production entity in the same way UK does) but a real one for any live-researched jurisdiction with a taxable-credit mechanism, and a real one conceptually: does "total spend" in a jurisdiction's own statutory definition include or exclude amounts the production expects to receive back? Get the order of operations wrong and the number silently drifts by a few percent in a way that's hard to catch by eyeballing.
**Prevention:** Compute in a fixed, explicit order for every jurisdiction: (1) determine qualifying base from spend, independent of the credit; (2) apply rate/tiers/caps to get gross credit; (3) apply payout-mechanism conversion (transfer discount, corporation tax on the credit itself, refund timing) to get net cash. Never let step 3's output feed back into step 1's input for the same production/jurisdiction pair. Write this as an explicit invariant/test: computing the same production twice through the pipeline should be idempotent.
**Milestone:** Accounts — core computation engine design, before any jurisdiction is coded.

#### A1.6 — Minimum spend thresholds treated as a smooth ramp rather than a cliff **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** Georgia's $500,000 minimum spend is almost certainly a hard cliff, not a phase-in: a $499,999 production likely qualifies for $0, and a $500,000 production qualifies for the full 20-30% on its entire qualifying base. New Mexico's CPA-audit-mandatory threshold ($5M requested credit) and its NRCE headcount sliding scale (5 positions up to $2.75M, 10 up to $7.5M, etc.) behave the same way — a $1 change in inputs can flip a discrete tier. A model built by smoothly interpolating between reference points, or one that doesn't test near-threshold inputs at all, will silently misprice any production sitting close to a cliff — and a demo production sized to look "typical" ($X million, round numbers) has a nontrivial chance of landing exactly near one of these cliffs by accident.
**Prevention:** Encode every threshold as a discrete step function, never an interpolation. Add explicit unit tests at cliff boundaries: one dollar below, exactly at, one dollar above, for every named threshold (GA $500K min spend, GA audit-fee tier boundaries at $5M/$10M, NM $5M audit trigger, NM headcount steps). The audit-fee tiers already named in the briefs ($500K–$5M → $5,000; $5–10M → $12,500; $10M+ → $25,000) are themselves cliffs and deserve the same test treatment.
**Milestone:** Accounts — jurisdiction rule-encoding; test suite is the deliverable, not just the formula.

#### A1.7 — Per-episode vs per-season treatment for series, and season aggregation vs single-title validation pairs **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** Two named validation pairs (Succession S4, The Gilded Age S2) are *season-level* aggregates, not single episodes — $102.9M qualified spend for a season, not an episode. If the internal budget model represents "shoot days" and "crew size" at a per-production granularity without a season/episode dimension, there is no way to correctly reproduce a season-aggregate validation figure, because per-episode caps (New Mexico explicitly gives more NRCE headcount slots "if a pilot goes to series," and the ≥6-episode threshold for TV Pilot/Series treatment) require knowing episode count, not just total spend.
**Prevention:** The physical-inputs schema needs an explicit "number of episodes in this order" field for series productions (already implied by "type and scale — feature/limited series/episodic" in idea-2-incentives.md, but not yet connected to any per-episode threshold logic). Validate specifically: can the model take Succession S4's $102.9M and reproduce $25.7M using season-level inputs alone, or does it require per-episode granularity the disclosed data doesn't provide? If the latter, this pair should be demoted from "exact match" to "directional match" expectation before the demo, not discovered live.
**Milestone:** Accounts — validation loop design (Job 1), before committing to which of the 11 sourced pairs are "exact match" targets vs "zone match" targets.

#### A1.8 — Sales tax and hotel occupancy tax exemptions omitted as a separate stackable benefit **[NEW, confirmed this session]**
**What goes wrong:** Multiple curated-adjacent jurisdictions offer production-specific sales tax exemptions (on tangible property and services used directly in production — confirmed for NY, TX) *and* separately, hotel occupancy tax exemptions or exclusions after a duration threshold (Connecticut: 12% hotel tax only applies to the first 30 consecutive days; New Jersey: occupancies of 90+ consecutive days exempt from the 5% Hotel Room Occupancy Tax; California: many localities exempt stays over 30 days; Virginia: exempt after 90 days). These are genuinely separate levers from the income/production tax credit and are easy to either double-count (treating them as part of the headline incentive) or omit entirely (undercounting true landed cost, which is the project's actual metric).
**Prevention:** Model these as distinct cost-reduction line items in the landed-cost calculation, not as part of the incentive-credit computation. Because they interact with shoot-day count and housing duration (which the system already collects), they are cheap to add correctly once the schema distinguishes "incentive value" from "other tax-driven cost reductions." Given the project's own headline metric is *total landed cost*, omitting these is a real accuracy gap, not a cosmetic one — a long-duration Connecticut shoot's hotel bill is materially overstated without the 30-day exemption applied.
**Milestone:** Accounts — cost model for housing/per-diem line items. Low complexity, moderate accuracy payoff; a good candidate to time-box rather than skip, since it directly serves the "true landed cost" claim.

#### A1.9 — Loan-out withholding modelled as a compliance footnote, not a cash cost **[EXTENDS the briefs' "loan-out triggers withholding" note]**
**What goes wrong:** The briefs correctly note that paying an actor via loan-out avoids the per-person qualifying cap but "triggers a separate GA withholding obligation." What they do not do is treat that withholding as a modelled cost that offsets the benefit. This session's research confirms these rates vary materially by state and are non-trivial: Georgia ~4.99% (see A1.10 below on resolving the 4.99%/5.75% conflict), New Jersey 6.37%, Massachusetts 5% (9% above $1M), Louisiana 3.09%, Kentucky 3.5%. On a $2M loan-out payment, Georgia's 4.99% withholding is a ~$100K cash outflow the production must fund (even if ultimately creditable against the loan-out entity's own tax liability, it is a timing and cash-flow cost, not a wash) — a nontrivial fraction of the incentive gain from avoiding the $500K W-2 cap in the first place. A model that shows loan-out as strictly better than W-2 by the full $1.5M difference in qualifying base, without netting the withholding cost, overstates the loan-out advantage.
**Prevention:** When the model chooses (or lets the user model) a loan-out payment structure, compute and display the withholding as an explicit cost line netted against the incentive gain, not just a footnote. This is exactly the kind of "second-order but real" number that a technically literate judge (line producer, accountant) would check for.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Georgia-adjacent rule encoding if Georgia is reached via Job 2; more importantly, a general principle to bake into the loan-out-vs-W-2 comparison logic wherever it's built.

#### A1.10 — Resolving the Georgia loan-out withholding conflict: 4.99% vs 5.75% **[EXTENDS an explicitly named open item — partial resolution found]**
**What goes wrong / new finding:** This session's research found vendor sources (aggregated tax-service summaries) converging on **4.99%** for Georgia loan-out withholding specifically. 4.99% matches Georgia's current flat personal income tax rate after its 2024 tax reform (effective for tax years starting 2024, with a further scheduled reduction path); 5.75% was Georgia's prior top marginal individual income tax bracket before that reform. **Working hypothesis: these are not two competing facts but two different vintages — 5.75% is the pre-2024 rate, 4.99% is current.** This is not independently verified against a Georgia Department of Revenue primary source in this session and should be confirmed against dor.georgia.gov before being coded as fact — but it demonstrates the *general pattern* worth generalizing: an apparent numeric conflict between two secondary sources is very often a recency artifact (rate changed, one source updated, one didn't), not a genuine live disagreement. See Part D below for the general policy this implies.
**Prevention:** Before coding the 4.99% figure, confirm against a dated Georgia DOR primary source (the mandatory-audit-fees page or a withholding-specific bulletin) and record the effective date. If confirmed, this resolves one of the two explicitly-named open items in PROJECT.md and productionfinance-brief.md.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Phase 0 (source verification), the phase PROJECT.md already designates for exactly this kind of reconciliation.

#### A1.11 — Time value of money ignored when comparing jurisdictions with different cash-arrival timing **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** The briefs correctly compute *net cash amount* by mechanism (refundable vs transferable vs taxable vs rebate) and correctly flag *when* the cash arrives, but nothing in the supplied material describes discounting delayed cash to make it comparable to fast cash. A jurisdiction paying 25% refundable in 8 weeks and a jurisdiction paying 28% refundable in 14 months are not offering equivalent value to a production financing against expected incentive proceeds — the second is worth less in present-value terms, and materially so given production financing costs typically run well above risk-free rates. Presenting both as flat net-cash percentages without a time adjustment silently biases the "ranking that inverts once net cash and timing are applied" (a named demo beat) toward whichever jurisdiction merely has the lower fee/discount, without capturing the financing-cost penalty of a long wait.
**Prevention:** Either (a) apply an explicit, clearly-labelled discount rate to delayed cash (even a simple stated annual rate, e.g., "assumes an 8% production financing cost, cash discounted to present value at the state's average arrival time") so the timing dimension actually moves the ranking, or (b) if time-boxing prevents implementing discounting, at minimum surface arrival time as its own sortable column so a user can reason about it themselves rather than have it silently folded into (or omitted from) the net-cash figure. Do not claim "we model timing" if timing is displayed but not actually priced into any ranking.
**Milestone:** Accounts — net-cash conversion logic. This is a good candidate for an honest MVP scope cut: ship (b) first, add (a) only if time remains, and say so explicitly in the written description ("timing is displayed, not yet discounted into the ranking") rather than implying more than is built.

## PART B — VALIDATION LOOP PITFALLS (Job 1)

### B1. Allocation-stage figures are not the same claim as issued figures **[EXTENDS a point already partially named]**
**What goes wrong:** The feasibility research already flags that CA and NJ figures are allocation/estimated-stage, while NY is audited/issued — but the practical risk is conflating them *inside the same validation-accuracy metric*. A "mean error across all validated pairs" figure that blends NY's issued-figure precision with CA's approval-stage estimate precision produces a headline number that overstates confidence in the CA/NJ portion and, if a CA production's actual issued credit later differs from its allocation (see B3), retroactively invalidates a "match" that was never really a match to a completed fact.
**Prevention:** Report validation accuracy *stratified by disclosure stage* (issued vs allocated vs estimated), not blended into one number. State explicitly, next to each validated pair, which stage it represents. This is a cheap, high-credibility move: "matches at the allocation stage" is a real and honestly-stated claim; a single blended "0.4% mean error" number that quietly mixes issued and allocated figures is not.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Job 1 design, and the public accuracy figure (also published in Balances per PROJECT.md Active — Balances).

### B2. CA/NJ allocation figures can change after publication — reallocation and non-completion **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** California's tax credit program allocates credits based on projected spend at approval; if a production doesn't proceed, is materially reduced in scope, or fails to meet its committed spend, the allocation can be reduced, forfeited, or reallocated to the next round. This wasn't independently confirmed against a specific CA statute clause in this session, but it is consistent with how every capped, application-based allocation programme works (the alternative — allocations that are never adjusted regardless of actual outcome — would defeat the purpose of a cap) and is exactly the kind of silent-drift risk the project's own validation loop is designed to catch for *rule changes*, but not obviously designed to catch for *this-specific-production-changed* cases.
**Prevention:** When ingesting a new CA/NJ disclosure batch, diff against the previous batch for the same production title/company, not just add new rows — a title whose allocation amount changed between two disclosure dates for the same production is a signal to re-derive against the newer figure, and a title that disappeared entirely between batches (withdrawn, terminated) should be flagged rather than silently left stale in the validation set.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Job 1, ingestion logic. Verify against a specific CA Film Commission FAQ or statute reference before hackathon submission if time allows; otherwise state the caveat explicitly in the written description.

### B3. The same production appearing across multiple disclosure batches, double-counted **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** A long-shooting production (a multi-season series, or a feature that spans a fiscal-year boundary) may appear in more than one quarterly NY ESD report, or in both a CA allocation batch and a later CA final-certification batch, representing different tranches of the same underlying production rather than independent validation events. If Job 1 naively treats "every row in every ingested report" as one independent test case, the accuracy metric silently double-counts productions that happen to disclose cleanly (inflating apparent accuracy on well-behaved, single-jurisdiction, single-tranche productions) relative to harder multi-jurisdiction or multi-year cases that appear once or not at all.
**Prevention:** De-duplicate by production title + company + jurisdiction before computing the headline accuracy metric; if a production legitimately appears across multiple tranches, treat that explicitly as a cumulative reconciliation case (sum the tranches, compare to the model's cumulative prediction) rather than as N independent samples.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Job 1, before the "tested against N government-disclosed awards, mean error X%" claim is finalized (this exact phrasing appears in idea-2-incentives.md and will be quoted in the demo).

### B4. Productions spanning multiple tax years or multiple jurisdictions have no single "the answer" to reproduce **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** A production that shoots partly in one calendar year and completes post in the next, or shoots across two jurisdictions in the curated set, doesn't have one clean "qualified spend → credit issued" pair to validate against — the disclosed figure may represent only one jurisdiction's or one year's slice of a larger production, while the system's physical-input model (a single "start window by quarter") represents the whole production as one atomic unit. Choosing this kind of production as a validation target without recognizing the mismatch produces a comparison that looks like a model bug but is actually a scope mismatch between what was disclosed and what the model computed.
**Prevention:** When selecting which of the 11 sourced pairs (and any newly ingested pairs) to use as headline "exact match" demo cases, actively screen for single-jurisdiction, single-tax-year productions (Anora is explicitly the clean case named in the briefs for exactly this reason — "small indies with no uplift claims are your best test cases"). Reserve multi-year/multi-jurisdiction productions for a labelled "directional match, here's why it's not exact" category rather than a silent miss.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Job 1 / demo script selection.

### B5. What a legitimate mismatch looks like vs a model bug — and how to report it **[NEW, directly requested]**
**What goes wrong if this isn't designed deliberately:** Without a stated policy, the system either (a) silently reports every mismatch as "estimate close enough," smoothing over real bugs, or (b) panics and labels every mismatch a failure, including the legitimate ones described in B1–B4 (allocation-stage estimates, unseen uplifts, season aggregation, multi-year splits) that are not model bugs at all — they're honest limits of what the disclosed data can support.
**Prevention — a defensible mismatch taxonomy, checkable in Job 1's output:**
1. **Exact match** (within rounding) — small, single-jurisdiction, single-year, no-uplift production, disclosure stage is issued/audited. Anora-class.
2. **Zone match, explained** — mismatch attributable to a *named, visible* unmodelled factor: an uplift claimed that isn't in the disclosed fields (Gilded Age's 26.3% vs Succession's 25.0% residue, already named in the briefs as exactly this case), allocation-vs-issued stage difference, or season aggregation. The system should state *which* named factor it believes explains the gap, not just report a percentage error.
3. **Unexplained mismatch** — the model's output differs from disclosure and none of the known categories explain it. **This must be surfaced, not hidden or averaged away** — it is either a real bug (rule encoded wrong, threshold misplaced) or a genuine rule change the validation loop hasn't caught up to yet (the change-detection mechanism the briefs already describe). Either way it belongs in the published accuracy figure as a distinct, visible category, not folded into a blended mean that dilutes it into invisibility.
**Test:** Before submission, deliberately break one jurisdiction's rule (e.g., swap a cap value) and confirm Job 1's reporting correctly classifies the resulting mismatches as "unexplained" rather than silently absorbing them into a still-plausible-looking mean-error number. If the system can't tell the difference between "we're right, this is a known limitation" and "we're wrong," the validation loop is decorative, not real.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Job 1 design and the Balances "publish the running accuracy figure" requirement; this taxonomy should be the schema for that figure, not an afterthought.

## PART C — LIVE-RESEARCH / LLM-EXTRACTION PITFALLS (Job 2)

All of Part C is **[NEW]** — the supplied briefs describe *what* Job 2 must do but not the concrete failure modes of the Parallel Search + Gemini extraction pipeline itself.

### C1. Extracting a superseded rate from an undated or archived page
**What goes wrong:** Search results and cached/archived government pages frequently lack a visible "last updated" date, or carry a stale one (the feasibility research notes exactly this problem for EP's incentives map — "no visible last-updated" — and for Cast & Crew's Canada map PDF, confirmed dated January 2020 despite being served as current). An extraction pipeline that pulls the first plausible-looking rate off a page without checking page-level or clause-level dating risks quoting a rate that a 2023 statute has already superseded.
**Guardrail:** Require the extraction step to locate and surface an explicit effective-date or last-amended marker from the page content itself (not the HTTP Last-Modified header, which reflects server caching, not content currency) before accepting a rate as current. If no date marker is found, the confidence tier must default to low, and the UI must say so rather than silently presenting a number with no visible caveat.

### C2. Secondary sources (law firm summaries, vendor marketing) outranking primary government pages in search results
**What goes wrong:** EP, Wrapbook, Cast & Crew and similar vendor content are well-SEO'd, frequently updated for lead-gen purposes, and often rank above the actual government statute or agency bulletin in a generic web search. A search-then-extract pipeline that takes the top result without a source-type filter will systematically prefer polished secondary summaries over primary text — exactly the inversion the project's own credibility claim depends on avoiding (the whole differentiator is "we cite the government document," not "we cite a vendor's incentive map").
**Guardrail:** Apply an explicit domain allowlist/tier at the search-query stage, not just at citation-display time — bias Parallel Search queries toward `.gov`, official agency domains, and known primary legislative/regulatory hosts first, and only fall back to vendor/press summaries when no primary source is retrievable, with the resulting confidence tier downgraded accordingly and the fact that it's a secondary source stated in the UI, not just linked quietly.

### C3. A PDF whose table structure defeats extraction
**What goes wrong:** The gold-standard validation source (NY ESD quarterly PDF reports) is itself a multi-column table PDF — exactly the format most prone to silent extraction errors: column misalignment, merged cells for multi-line production names, numbers that get associated with the wrong row when a table spans a page break. A silently-misaligned extraction could pull a real number from the PDF but attach it to the wrong production, which would then fail validation for reasons that look like a modelling bug (see B5) but are actually a parsing bug.
**Guardrail:** For any extraction from a tabular PDF used as validation ground truth, spot-check a fixed number of hand-verified rows (the Anora, Succession, Gilded Age figures already manually transcribed in feasibility-incentives.md are ideal known-good anchors) against the automated extraction on every ingestion run, and fail loudly if the automated figure for a known-good row doesn't match the manually-verified one. This turns the existing 11 sourced pairs into a built-in extraction regression test, essentially for free.

### C4. Hallucinated specificity — a plausible-looking rate with no traceable source
**What goes wrong:** LLM extraction/summarization steps can produce a confident, specific-sounding figure ("Georgia's rate is 20% base, 30% with uplift, capped at $10M") that blends real facts from training data with page content in a way that sounds sourced but isn't traceable to the specific page fetched in that run. This is the single most dangerous failure mode for a project whose entire premise is "every figure carries a source link and the date it was checked" (PROJECT.md, Active — Accounts) — a hallucinated number with a plausible-looking citation attached is worse than an admitted "not found," because it looks validated.
**Guardrail:** Structurally prevent the extraction step from emitting a numeric claim that isn't grounded in a specific retrieved passage — require the extraction call to return the rate *and* a verbatim quote or span from the source document containing that rate, and reject/flag any output where the quote can't be located in the actually-fetched content (a simple substring/fuzzy-match check against the fetched page text, not just trusting the model's self-report). This is checkable mechanically and should be a hard gate, not a prompt instruction alone (prompting an LLM to "only state facts you can source" reduces but does not eliminate this failure mode).

### C5. Conflating a proposed bill with enacted law
**What goes wrong:** Named explicitly as a live, unresolved risk in the source research — New Mexico HB 237, a *proposed* repeal, this session's research confirms it was introduced (2025) with no located confirmation of final enacted outcome as of the current date. A page describing "HB 237 would repeal the film incentive" is easy to extract as "New Mexico is repealing its film incentive" if the extraction step doesn't distinguish bill status language ("introduced," "passed committee," "signed into law," "enacted," "effective date") from outcome.
**Guardrail:** Require the extraction step to classify bill/legislation status explicitly (introduced / passed one chamber / passed both / signed / enacted / effective) whenever the source text uses any of those status words, and refuse to update a jurisdiction's live rule set on anything short of "signed" or "enacted." Any live-researched jurisdiction whose most recent primary-source hit is bill-status language, not statute language, should surface as "pending change — not yet in effect" rather than silently updating the number. Verify HB 237's actual current status against nmlegis.gov before any NM-adjacent live demo, given the explicit live-risk flag already raised in the source material.

### C6. Currency and locale number-format misparsing
**What goes wrong:** European/UK sources and any jurisdiction using period-as-thousands-separator formatting (e.g., "1.000,00" rather than "1,000.00") risk a factor-of-1000 or decimal-place misparse if extraction assumes US number formatting universally. Given the UK worked example (£18M) is a named demo beat and any live-researched European jurisdiction (Hungary, Czech Republic, Spain — all named in the feasibility research as live-fire or unverified) would hit this directly, a locale-blind parser is a real risk, not a theoretical one.
**Guardrail:** Detect currency symbol/locale from the source page (a `.uk`/`.eu` government domain, or an explicit currency symbol in the text) and apply locale-aware number parsing rather than a single hardcoded format; add a unit test with a European-formatted number literal to the extraction pipeline's test suite specifically, since this class of bug produces a wrong-by-orders-of-magnitude number that's easy to miss on casual inspection (a number that's off by 1000x can still look "plausible" if nobody double-checks it against context).

### C7. Confusing a film-specific tax credit with an unrelated general business-investment credit
**What goes wrong:** Government tax sites frequently list a state's film incentive alongside unrelated economic-development or investment tax credits (job-creation credits, opportunity-zone credits, general manufacturing incentives) on the same page or in the same search-result cluster, especially for smaller/less-documented jurisdictions reached only via Job 2. An extraction step keying loosely on "tax credit" + jurisdiction name risks pulling in the wrong programme's rate or cap entirely.
**Guardrail:** Require the extraction/search query to anchor on film/television/media-production-specific programme names or statutory citations (not just "the state's tax credit"), and have the system state which named programme (by its official title, e.g., "Georgia Entertainment Industry Investment Act" not just "Georgia's credit") it believes it found, surfaced in the UI so a knowledgeable user can immediately spot a wrong-programme match.

### C8. How low confidence should be surfaced, not smoothed over
**General guardrail across C1–C7:** Every one of the above failure modes degrades gracefully into the *same* correct behaviour if the confidence-tier system is taken seriously: when any of C1 (no date found), C2 (secondary source only), C3 (extraction couldn't be spot-checked), C4 (no groundable quote), C5 (bill not yet enacted), C6 (locale ambiguous), or C7 (programme identity uncertain) fires, the output for that figure should be explicitly downgraded and *visually distinguished*, not merely tagged in a database field nobody surfaces (see Part F, Credibility Trap 1, for why visual weight matters as much as the underlying tag). A live-researched city returning "no programme found" is explicitly named in the brief as an *acceptable* result — the system should be equally comfortable returning "found a programme, but confidence is low, here's exactly why" rather than forcing every live-research result into a binary found/not-found frame that hides the C1–C7 nuance.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Job 2 design and the shared confidence-tier UI component (used by both curated and live-researched paths).

## PART D — CONFLICTING SOURCES: A DEFENSIBLE RESOLUTION POLICY

### D1. The NY $700M/$800M "conflict" is very likely not a conflict at all **[EXTENDS the explicitly-named open item — resolution found this session]**
This session's research found the likely resolution: **$700M is the base NY film/TV/post credit programme, extended through 2036; $800M is that $700M plus a newly created, separately-structured $100M independent film pool** (itself split $20M for sub-$10M-budget films and $80M for larger indies, per the FY2026 budget). Multiple sources (Wrapbook, Entertainment Partners, Hollywood Reporter) independently describe it this way, converging on the same decomposition. **This should still be confirmed against the enacted budget bill text or a tax.ny.gov primary source before being coded as fact** — but it is very likely resolvable, not genuinely contradictory. Both cited figures may be correct simultaneously, describing different aggregation scopes.
**The general lesson [NEW]:** what looks like "two sources disagree" is very often "two sources describe different scopes, at different points in time, or using different units" — not a live factual dispute. A resolution policy needs a step that checks for this *before* declaring an unresolved conflict, because declaring a false conflict is itself a credibility cost (it signals sloppier research than confirming the actual, more nuanced truth).

### D2. A defensible resolution policy — precedence ordering
When two sources genuinely disagree (after ruling out D1's "different scope" explanation), apply this ordering, in priority:
1. **Enacted statutory text** (the actual bill/session law as codified) over any agency summary, press release, or third-party description of it.
2. **The primary regulator's own current guidance page/PDF** (e.g., an ESD or DOR-published guidelines document with a visible revision date) over vendor or press summaries.
3. **More recent** over older, *when both are dated and comparably authoritative* — but recency alone should never override statutory text with a lower priority-tier source.
4. **Multiple independent secondary sources converging** (as happened with the NY figure this session — three unrelated outlets describing the same decomposition) is meaningful corroboration, but is still lower-priority than finding the primary source directly, and should be labelled as "corroborated via secondary sources, primary source not directly confirmed" rather than presented with the same confidence as a directly-read statute.
5. **If the primary source itself is genuinely ambiguous or internally inconsistent** (not just "hard to find") — this is a true unresolved conflict, and should be displayed as such, not resolved by fiat.

### D3. Displaying an unresolved conflict rather than silently picking one **[NEW, directly requested]**
**What to do instead of guessing:** When D2's ordering doesn't resolve to a single confident answer, the UI should show both figures with their respective sources and dates, and a plain-language note ("NY's own materials describe both a $700M base programme and an $800M combined figure including a new independent-film pool — we use $700M for [reason] because [X]" or, if genuinely unresolved, "sources disagree and we could not confirm which is current as of [date checked] — treating as unresolved"). This is strictly better for credibility than silently picking one number and hoping nobody checks, precisely because the audience for this project (per PROJECT.md) explicitly includes people who know the business and will check.
**Test:** Before submission, deliberately audit every place in the curated jurisdiction models where two source figures were found and only one was kept — for each one, confirm there's a documented resolution reason (per D2's ordering) rather than an arbitrary pick. This is a good candidate for a literal checklist item during the Phase 0 source-verification work PROJECT.md already schedules.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Phase 0 (source verification) for the resolution itself; the shared confidence-tier UI component for the display mechanism (same component that serves Part C's low-confidence surfacing).

## PART E — COST-MODEL TRAPS

### E1. Published union rate cards are not what a production actually pays **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** IATSE/SAG-AFTRA/DGA/WGA/BECTU/ACTRA published rate cards give the base wage scale, but the real cost to a production includes fringe/payroll burden on top — pension, health & welfare contributions, payroll taxes (FICA/FUTA/SUTA equivalents), workers' compensation insurance — which industry practice commonly estimates in the range of roughly 30-40%+ on top of base wages depending on union and state (this figure is an industry-common planning heuristic, not independently verified against a specific union's current fringe schedule in this session, and should be sourced properly before being hardcoded). A model that applies base rate card wages directly as "labour cost" without a fringe/burden multiplier will understate labour cost across every city, which — because the fringe burden rate itself varies by union and somewhat by state — also distorts the *relative* comparison between cities, not just the absolute numbers.
**Prevention:** Apply an explicit, sourced fringe/burden multiplier on top of base wage-card rates, sourced per union where possible (IATSE locals publish fringe rates in their own agreements, not just base scale) rather than a single blanket estimate, and disclose the multiplier used as its own line item — this is directly analogous to the transfer-discount and audit-fee treatment already correctly modelled for incentives, and the same "show your work" principle should extend to the cost side, not just the incentive side.
**Milestone:** Accounts — cost model for labour, built alongside the union rate card ingestion.

### E2. Overtime, turnaround penalties, meal penalties and kit fees ignored **[NEW]**
**What goes wrong:** Actual production labour cost is driven substantially by overtime (a >8 or >10 hour day, common in the industry), turnaround violations (insufficient rest between calls, triggering a penalty payment under most union agreements), meal penalties (working through a meal break), and kit fees (crew members renting their own gear to the production, common for grip/electric/sound/costume). None of these are captured by a flat day-rate × shoot-days calculation, and they are not evenly distributed across cities — a location with tighter daylight windows or longer commute-driven turnarounds will systematically generate more of these costs than one that doesn't, which is directly relevant to the "why is everyone leaving town" comparison the project's headline output claims to answer.
**Prevention:** Given the 17-day timeline, this is very unlikely to be fully modelled — the honest response is to treat it as a named, disclosed simplification: state explicitly in the UI and the written description that the labour line uses base+fringe rate-card costs and does not currently model overtime/turnaround/meal-penalty/kit-fee variance, so the reader can weight the "landed cost" figure accordingly. This is a defensible scope cut only if disclosed; it is a credibility trap (see Part F) if presented as if the labour figure already accounts for it.
**Milestone:** Accounts, as a documented limitation — candidate for Balances if time allows a v2 refinement, but do not silently claim more precision than the model has.

### E3. Non-union local rates are not public, and the model cannot pretend otherwise **[EXTENDS the briefs' explicit non-goal]**
**What goes wrong:** PROJECT.md already correctly excludes non-union local labour rates as out of scope ("not public — that is what Entertainment Partners and Cast & Crew sell"). The trap is in execution, not in the decision: any temptation, under deadline pressure, to approximate non-union local rates using a rough regional cost-of-living multiplier applied to union scale, and present the result without flagging it as an estimate rather than a sourced figure, would violate the confidence-tier system (PROJECT.md: "carry a confidence tier on every figure: validated or researched" — an unsourced multiplier-based guess is neither).
**Prevention:** If a non-union-heavy production type needs to be priced (documentaries, some indie features, reality/competition formats), either restrict the physical inputs to imply union coverage by default and disclose that restriction, or clearly separate any non-union estimate into its own low-confidence, explicitly-labelled-as-modelled (not researched, not validated) category with the estimation method shown.
**Milestone:** Accounts — input schema, confidence-tier design.

### E4. GSA per diem used as a market hotel-rate proxy without the ceiling-vs-market caveat **[NEW, directly requested]**
**What goes wrong:** GSA per diem (and State Department foreign per diem) are federal *government reimbursement ceilings*, calculated as an average of surveyed mid-range hotel pricing for a defined geography — not a guaranteed or typical rate a production will actually pay for the volume, timing, and quality of rooms a real shoot needs. This session's research confirms the gap is real and documented: production-industry commentary explicitly notes the gap between GSA rates and actual market conditions has widened, "forcing project managers to choose between overworking crews with long commutes, blowing the budget, or risking tax mistakes" — and that even the GSA's own base lodging rate excludes local hotel/occupancy taxes and mandatory fees, which in some counties add 12%+ on top. Using the raw GSA figure as if it were the true expected hotel cost will systematically understate housing cost for imported crew, especially in high-demand production markets or during peak season, and — because the gap size varies by market — will also distort the *relative* comparison between cities.
**Is it defensible as a proxy? — With a stated caveat, yes; unstated, no.** GSA per diem is free, authoritative, granular (by county, monthly for foreign per diem), and — crucially — is the number actually referenced by many production accounting departments as a *planning floor*, not because it's realistic, but because it's the only free structured number that exists (this is the same "no API/no bulk data" reality that justifies the incentive-side research approach). Using it is reasonable *if the UI explicitly states it is a reimbursement ceiling, not a market rate, and that actual housing cost is likely higher, especially in peak season or tight-inventory markets*.
**Prevention:** Label the housing/per-diem cost line explicitly as "GSA/State Department per diem (federal reimbursement rate, not a guaranteed market rate — actual cost may be higher)" wherever it's displayed, rather than presenting it with the same unqualified confidence as a sourced union rate card figure. This is a cheap, one-sentence fix that directly prevents a credibility hit from anyone in the industry who knows per diem isn't a hotel rate.
**Milestone:** Accounts — cost model, housing/per-diem line item and its confidence-tier labelling.

### E5. The honest way to model a cost line whose real inputs are not public **[NEW, general principle synthesizing E1-E4]**
Every cost-model trap above shares one root cause: several of the biggest cost lines in a real production budget (non-union labour, actual hotel negotiated rates, kit fees, overtime patterns) simply are not public data anywhere, for the same structural reason incentive rules aren't API-accessible — the difference is that for costs, there is no government disclosure requirement standing in for a bulk source at all. The honest approach, consistent with the project's own stated principle of never presenting a researched figure as validated: **every cost line should carry the same confidence-tier discipline as incentive figures** — sourced-and-current (union scale, GSA per diem, published stage/equipment rate cards), estimated-with-disclosed-method (fringe multiplier, regional cost-of-living adjustment), or acknowledged-gap (overtime/turnaround/kit fees, non-union rates) — displayed with equal visual honesty. Do not let the "confidence tier" system apply only to the incentive half of the output and default to unlabelled implied-precision on the cost half, since cost is the majority of the headline "total landed cost" number.
**Milestone:** Accounts — this should be a cross-cutting review item at the end of the cost-model build phase, not a per-line-item afterthought.

## PART F — CREDIBILITY TRAPS

### F1. Presenting a researched figure with the same visual weight as a validated one **[NEW, directly requested]**
**What goes wrong:** PROJECT.md already mandates a confidence tier on every figure (validated/researched), but a tag in a data model does nothing if the UI renders both tiers in the same font size, colour, and prominence. A ranked list where a live-researched Bristol sits visually indistinguishable from a validated New York invites exactly the misreading the tiering system exists to prevent, and a technically literate viewer who later discovers the difference only via a tooltip or footnote will read that as concealment, not honesty.
**Prevention:** Make the confidence tier a first-class, always-visible visual signal (colour, icon, or explicit label directly on the number, not just in a hover state or detail panel) — every place a dollar figure appears, not just in a legend. Test: screenshot the ranked list and the decomposed-gap view; can a viewer tell validated from researched without clicking anything?
**Milestone:** Accounts — UI design, applies across the whole interface, not a single component.

### F2. Implying freshness the refresh cadence does not support **[EXTENDS an explicit non-goal, execution-level trap]**
**What goes wrong:** PROJECT.md already excludes "claiming live daily research as the product differentiator" — but the trap survives at the UI-copy level even after the strategic decision is made correctly: a "checked today" or "last updated: [today's date]" stamp on every figure, refreshed simply because the page was viewed today, implies a freshness the tiered-refresh design (FX daily, cap-consumption daily-weekly, rules monthly+event-driven, union rate cards annual) does not actually deliver for most figures. A viewer has no way to distinguish "this number was actually re-verified today" from "this page was rendered today, using a number cached weeks ago."
**Prevention:** Display the *actual* date the underlying figure was checked/verified (per the tiered-refresh cadence table already designed), not the page-render date. This is a small, mechanical distinction with an outsized credibility payoff, and it is exactly the kind of detail a technically literate viewer checks first.
**Milestone:** Accounts — every figure's citation display; a single shared component, get it right once.

### F3. Publishing an economic-impact figure without exposing its multiplier assumption **[EXTENDS an explicit non-goal — execution-level trap]**
**What goes wrong:** PROJECT.md already excludes ancillary economic-impact/ROI figures as out of scope, correctly citing that industry-funded studies and independent state auditors (Georgia's own included) disagree sharply. The trap is in incidental inclusion under deadline pressure: a demo script, a written-description paragraph, or a stray UI element that quotes a state's own "$X billion economic impact" press-release figure (several are floating in this session's own search results — e.g., a governor's office press release claiming "$1.1 billion to California's economy," "$6.6 billion in economic impact") *as if it were the project's own validated output*, without attribution or a multiplier caveat, imports exactly the politically-contested claim the project deliberately avoids making itself.
**Prevention:** Treat any economic-impact number sourced from a government press release the same as any other non-primary, non-computed figure — if it appears anywhere in the product (even as flavour text or context), it must be explicitly attributed as "the state's own claimed figure, multiplier assumption not independently verified," never presented adjacent to the project's own validated/researched figures without that distinction being visually obvious.
**Milestone:** Accounts — a review pass on all UI copy and the written description before submission, specifically hunting for any economic-impact language that crept in from source material during research or demo-scripting.

### F4. Any claim of accuracy not backed by a checkable document **[NEW, general synthesis]**
**What goes wrong:** The project's single biggest asset is that its accuracy claim is independently checkable — Anora's $991,190 is on a public PDF anyone can open. The trap is scope creep in the claim: once the validation loop has, say, 20 pairs from NY/CA/NJ/CT and a computed mean-error figure, it becomes tempting to describe the tool's overall accuracy in language that implies coverage it doesn't have ("we're accurate to within X%" without the qualifier "...for the four jurisdictions where government disclosure exists, and not independently checkable elsewhere").
**Prevention:** Every accuracy claim, in the demo video, written description, and UI, should carry its scope explicitly — "validated against N government-disclosed awards in NY/CA/NJ/CT" not "validated." This single discipline — always naming the denominator and the jurisdictions — is what separates the project's actual claim from the overclaiming the source research explicitly warns against ("overclaiming freshness invites a judge to check and disprove it" generalizes directly to overclaiming accuracy scope).
**Milestone:** Accounts and Balances both — this is a standing discipline for every place an accuracy number is quoted, including the Balances "publish the running accuracy figure" requirement.

## PART G — HACKATHON DELIVERY TRAPS

All items below map to Stage One (pass/fail, partly automated) or to concrete, checkable Stage Two risks. Framed as: failure mode → the specific pre-submission check that catches it.

### G1. Google Cloud SDK imported but not actually exercised at runtime
**Failure mode:** `google-genai` (or `google-adk`/`google-generativeai`/`google-cloud-aiplatform`) appears in `requirements.txt`/imports, but the actual code path that calls it is unreachable from the deployed app (a leftover dev/test script, a feature-flagged path that's off by default, or a call that's stubbed/mocked in the deployed environment for latency reasons).
**Check:** Trace, from the hosted URL's actual request path, to the specific line of code that constructs and calls the Google Gen AI client, and confirm it executes on a real user request in the deployed environment — not just in a local test. Add a visible, timestamped log line or trace on every real Gemini call, and confirm at least one appears in production logs during a live anonymous-session smoke test immediately before submission.

### G2. Parallel Search called only in a stub/demo path, not the real user flow
**Failure mode:** Given the latency and reliability risk (Critical Pitfall 5), a natural but disqualifying shortcut is wiring Job 2's live-research demo to a pre-recorded/cached response for reliability, while leaving the "real" Parallel Search call technically present in the codebase but not what a live anonymous user actually triggers.
**Check:** Same discipline as G1 — confirm from the hosted URL's real code path (not a demo-only branch) that entering an uncurated city triggers an actual Parallel Search API call. It's acceptable, and arguably wise given the deadline (see G5's fallback discussion), to have a *separate, clearly-labelled* fallback for demo-video recording reliability, but the primary hosted app must genuinely call Parallel — that's the Stage One bar, not the video's bar.

### G3. OSI license present as a file but not detectable in the GitHub About section
**Failure mode:** A `LICENSE` file exists in the repo root but GitHub's automated license detector doesn't pick it up (non-standard filename like `LICENSE.txt` in a non-root location, a license file with extra preamble text that confuses the detector, or a repo where the license was added but the About-section metadata was never refreshed/cached).
**Check:** After adding the license, visit the repo's public GitHub page (not just the file listing) and confirm the license badge/name appears in the About sidebar itself, not just that the file exists — this is a distinct check from "the file is there." Use a standard, unmodified OSI-license template (MIT/Apache-2.0/etc.) with no added preamble text, named exactly `LICENSE` (or `LICENSE.md`) at repo root, which is the most reliably auto-detected form.

### G4. Hosted URL fails for an anonymous, cold visitor
**Failure mode (four sub-modes, all real):**
- **Cold-start timeout** — first-request latency on a scaled-to-zero backend exceeds whatever timeout the judge's browser or the platform imposes, especially if the request also has to make a live Parallel/Gemini call as part of first load.
- **Auth wall left in from development** — a login/API-key gate never removed, or a CORS-restricted allowlist that only includes localhost/dev domains.
- **CORS failure between separately-hosted frontend and backend** — works from the same machine/network during development, fails from an external judge's browser hitting a different origin.
- **Region mismatch** — backend deployed in a region far from where automated Stage One checks or judges are located, amplifying latency into apparent failure.
**Check:** Test the exact hosted URL from a fully logged-out browser session, on a different network than the development machine (e.g., a phone on cellular data, or an incognito window with all cookies cleared), performed as the literal last step before submission — not "it worked when I built it," but "it worked cold, from a stranger's vantage point, minutes before the deadline."

### G5. Demo video over 3 minutes, or not showing the thing actually functioning
**Failure mode:** A cinematic/narrated walkthrough that explains the idea without showing a real request hitting the real hosted app (mirroring exactly the "no open input, no live variability" approach the sibling IBM project's own brief describes for its own different reasons) would undercut this project's specific requirement that open city input *is* the agentic demonstration and must be shown working, not narrated about. Given Critical Pitfall #5 (Job 2 live-research reliability), there's a real temptation to fake or narrate over the live-research beat rather than show it running.
**Check:** Time the video with a stopwatch, not an estimate. For the live-research beat specifically, record several actual live takes in advance and select the best clean one for the final cut — this is legitimate video editing, not the "sleep() behind a progress bar" dishonesty the constraints explicitly forbid, because the underlying system genuinely did run live; only the *recording* is chosen after the fact, not faked.

### G6. Commit history predating the 2026-07-27 contest window
**Failure mode:** Reusing any file, snippet, boilerplate, or even a `git init` history from before the contest opened — including something as innocuous as importing a personal starter template repo with its own pre-existing commit history — could read as "extending prior work."
**Check:** Confirm the repository's first commit timestamp is on or after 2026-07-27 before submission; if any pre-existing code is reused (e.g., a personal boilerplate), either recreate it fresh inside the contest window or explicitly disclose the reuse and confirm it doesn't violate "new code only."

### G7. Accidentally including a disallowed AI dependency transitively
**Failure mode:** The direct dependencies (`google-generativeai`/`google-genai`/`google-adk`/`google-cloud-aiplatform`) are compliant on their own, but a convenience/orchestration package pulled in for agent-workflow scaffolding is not. The specific, concrete risk this session's research surfaced: **`google-adk` supports a multi-model integration layer (`LiteLlm`) that can pull in `litellm`, which in turn can depend on or optionally install `openai` and other provider clients** — a plausible way to end up with a disallowed vendor's SDK installed in the dependency tree even though the code never calls it. Similarly, any `langchain`-family package (even a Google-specific one like `langchain-google-genai`) risks pulling in broader `langchain` core packages that carry their own multi-provider dependency surface, and "agent framework" is separately and explicitly disallowed regardless of which model it's configured to call.
**Check:** Run `pip show <package>` / inspect `requirements.txt` (or better, `pip list` in the actual deployed virtualenv, not just the top-level requirements file) and grep the full resolved dependency tree for `openai`, `anthropic`, `langchain`, `litellm`, `crewai`, `autogen`, and similar names — not just the direct imports. If any appear, even unused, either pin/exclude them explicitly (e.g., avoid `LiteLlm` model wrappers in `google-adk`, use its native Gemini model classes directly) or be prepared to justify their presence as an unused transitive artifact, which is a risk not worth carrying into a partly-automated Stage One check.

## PART H — SCOPE AND SCHEDULE TRAPS

### H1. Four curated jurisdictions may be the wrong number to protect the interface **[NEW, directly requested assessment]**
**Assessment:** NY, CA, NJ, CT is well-justified by validation coverage (the only four with per-production disclosure) and clears the "reproduce at least three published award figures" Definition of Done with margin (one to spare). But the real risk isn't the *choice* of four — it's treating all four as equally cheap to build. NY is the richest-documented and highest-priority (it's the demo's opening beat and the gold-standard validation source — see Critical Pitfall #1). CT has the least-verified structure (its CSV column headers are an explicitly named unresolved open item) and the smallest program profile of the four. **Recommendation: sequence NY first and treat it as unmissable; treat CT as the jurisdiction most defensible to descope to "live-researched, not curated" if time runs out**, since (a) its validation value is real but marginal on top of NY/CA/NJ already clearing the three-award bar, and (b) descoping it to Job 2's live-research path still demonstrates the system's capability, just without the curated confidence tier. Do not let CT's unresolved CSV-schema question consume days that belong to NY's rule-encoding.
**Milestone:** Accounts — sequencing decision, ideally locked at the roadmap stage, not discovered mid-build.

### H2. Normalization work expands without a stated boundary **[EXTENDS Critical Pitfall #3 with a concrete stopping rule]**
**Prevention, concretely:** Define, in writing, *before* jurisdiction rule-encoding starts, a fixed list of the rule dimensions the model will represent for the four curated jurisdictions (base definition, per-person caps, tiers/uplifts with their stacking rules, payout mechanism, the specific caps and thresholds already documented in feasibility-incentives.md) — and explicitly exclude anything not on that list as a stated, disclosed simplification (this document's E2 on overtime/turnaround is a template for how to state a simplification honestly). The single biggest schedule risk on a research-heavy project is the absence of a firm "this is in scope, this is not, and here's how we'll say so" line drawn before the deep-research instinct takes over.
**Check:** A written, dated scope-freeze note for each jurisdiction's rule set, checked off before moving to the next jurisdiction — not "we'll come back and refine this."

### H3. The live-research demo fallback needs to be decided now, not discovered under pressure **[EXTENDS Critical Pitfall #5]**
**Prevention:** Decide explicitly, at the planning stage, what happens if Job 2 is flaky on demo day: (a) the hosted app's live behaviour genuinely calls Parallel/Gemini (required for Stage One, see G2), but (b) the *demo video* uses a pre-recorded, genuinely-live take selected from several attempts (legitimate, see G5), and (c) if judges interact with the hosted URL directly and hit a slow/flaky live-research response, the UI should show honest, informative loading/error states ("researching Bristol, England — checking BFI and HMRC sources...", or "no programme found for [X] — this is expected for some cities") rather than a spinner that might time out silently. None of this requires faking anything; it requires designing for graceful degradation of a genuinely-live feature, which is different from faking liveness.
**Milestone:** Accounts — Job 2 UX design, decided before the final week, not improvised during demo rehearsal.

### H4. The public index (Balances) is genuinely at risk of not landing, and needs a minimum publishable version defined now **[NEW, directly requested assessment]**
**Assessment:** Balances depends on Accounts' engine being complete and correct first (PROJECT.md sequences it as Milestone 2), and Accounts alone already satisfies the hackathon's hosted-URL Definition of Done — meaning Balances is, correctly, not required for a valid submission. Given 17 days total and the depth of Accounts' own critical pitfalls (validation correctness, live-research reliability, interface quality), Balances is the highest-probability item to be cut or shipped in a minimal state.
**Minimum publishable version, if time is tight:** (1) the fixed reference production, priced across the fixed city set, computed and displayed as a static or manually-triggered run rather than a fully automated schedule — a scheduler is an infrastructure nicety, not the substance of the claim; (2) a change log with at least one real dated entry (even from the Accounts-phase validation runs, repurposed) rather than an empty "check back later" state; (3) the running accuracy figure from Job 1, which is largely already-built-in-Accounts data being republished, not new computation; (4) reverse mode can be dropped entirely from the minimum version — it's explicitly deferred in PROJECT.md's own Key Decisions and is the single most computationally novel remaining piece, making it the correct first cut if Balances time is short.
**Milestone:** Balances — define this minimum version explicitly as the fallback target before starting Balances work, so a partial Balances still ships something coherent rather than an empty shell.

| Skip time-value discounting on delayed cash (A1.11) | Saves nontrivial computation and design time | Ranking "inverts on net cash and timing" demo beat is weaker than claimed | Acceptable in Milestone 1 only if arrival timing is still displayed as its own column and the limitation is stated in the written description |
| Blend allocation-stage (CA/NJ) and issued-stage (NY) figures into one accuracy mean (B1) | Simpler headline number | Overstates confidence in the weaker-evidence jurisdictions; a challenge from a knowledgeable viewer is easy to lose | Never — stratify the metric, it costs a label, not a rebuild |
| Approximate non-union local labour with a cost-of-living multiplier on union scale (E3) | Fills an otherwise-empty cost line for non-union productions | Contradicts PROJECT.md's own explicit non-goal if presented without a loud "estimated, not researched" label | Only for a clearly-labelled, low-confidence-tier estimate, never presented as researched |
| Fake the live-research call for demo-day reliability (Critical Pitfall #5, G2, H3) | Removes the single biggest demo-day risk | Fails Stage One if the *hosted app itself* isn't calling Parallel live, and is exactly the dishonesty the constraints explicitly forbid | Never for the hosted app; acceptable only for video-editing purposes (select the best of several genuinely-live recorded takes) |

## Integration Gotchas

| Integration | Common Mistake | Correct Approach |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Parallel Search API | Calling it only from a demo/dev script, not the deployed request path (G2) | Trace a real anonymous-user request end to end and confirm the API call fires in production logs |
| Gemini / google-genai | Importing but routing around it under latency pressure (G1) | Log every real call with a timestamp; confirm at least one appears during a pre-submission cold smoke test |
| google-adk multi-model layer (LiteLlm) | Pulling in `litellm`, which can transitively install `openai` (G7) | Use google-adk's native Gemini model classes directly; audit the full resolved dependency tree, not just direct imports |
| NY ESD quarterly PDF ingestion | Treating every row as an independent validation sample without de-duplicating multi-tranche productions (B3) | De-duplicate by production+jurisdiction before computing the headline accuracy metric |
| CA Film Commission approved-projects list | Treating an allocation-stage figure as equivalent evidence to NY's issued figure (B1) | Stratify and label validation accuracy by disclosure stage |
| GSA/State Dept per diem | Using the raw figure as an implied market hotel rate with no caveat (E4) | Label explicitly as a reimbursement ceiling, not a guaranteed market rate |

## Performance Traps

| Trap | Symptoms | Prevention | When It Breaks |
|------|----------|------------|-----------------|
| Live Parallel Search + Gemini extraction chained synchronously into a single user-facing request for Job 2 | First response for an uncurated city takes tens of seconds; judge perceives it as hung | Show explicit progressive status ("searching... found BFI page... extracting rate...") rather than a blank spinner; consider async/polling if the chain is long | Any uncurated city with a multi-hop research chain (national + regional programme lookup) |
| Cold-start on a scaled-to-zero hosted backend, compounded by a first-request live API chain | Anonymous visitor's first load times out or appears broken (G4) | Keep a warm instance during the judging window if the hosting platform allows it, or separate a fast static shell from the slower live-research path so the app never appears fully hung | Any request from a genuinely-cold instance |

## Security Mistakes

| Mistake | Risk | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| Leaving a development auth gate or hardcoded API key/allowlist in the deployed hosted URL (G4) | Anonymous judge locked out — a Stage One failure, not just a UX flaw | Explicit pre-submission checklist item: test cold, logged-out, from a different network |
| Committing a Parallel/Google Cloud API key directly into the public repository | Key leakage, and a visible red flag to any judge browsing the public repo (which the constraints explicitly say is inspectable) | Use environment variables / secret manager from day one; scan the final commit history for accidental key commits before submission |

## UX Pitfalls

| Pitfall | User Impact | Better Approach |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| Confidence tiers exist in data but not visually (F1) | Producer/judge can't tell validated from researched at a glance, undermining the core credibility claim | Persistent, always-visible visual signal on every figure, not a hover-only tooltip |
| "Checked today" implying re-verification that didn't happen (F2) | Overstates freshness; disprovable by a judge who checks a source date | Show the actual last-verified date per the tiered-refresh cadence |
| Binary found/not-found framing for live research hides C1-C7 nuance (C8) | A low-confidence but present figure looks as solid as a clean miss or a clean hit | Three-way (or richer) confidence surfacing: validated / researched-high-confidence / researched-low-confidence-because-X / not found |
| Silent unexplained mismatches folded into a blended accuracy mean (B5) | A real bug hides inside a still-plausible-looking headline number | Explicit "unexplained mismatch" category, visible in the published accuracy figure |

## "Looks Done But Isn't" Checklist

- [ ] **Google Cloud SDK integration:** Often "present in imports" but not exercised by the real deployed request path — verify with a production log line on a live anonymous smoke test (G1).
- [ ] **Parallel Search integration:** Often wired only into a demo/dev branch — verify the hosted app's actual Job 2 code path fires a real API call (G2).
- [ ] **OSI license:** Often a file in the repo but not detected in the GitHub About sidebar — check the rendered repo page, not just the file tree (G3).
- [ ] **Validation accuracy figure:** Often a single blended mean-error number — verify it's stratified by disclosure stage (issued/allocated/estimated) and separates explained-zone-matches from unexplained mismatches (B1, B5).
- [ ] **Confidence tiers:** Often present as a data field but invisible in the rendered UI — verify visually, not just in the schema (F1).
- [ ] **Cost-model completeness:** Often silently missing fringe/payroll burden, overtime, and GSA-as-ceiling caveats — verify every cost line carries the same sourced/estimated/gap-labelled discipline as the incentive side (E1-E5).
- [ ] **Hosted URL for anonymous visitors:** Often "works for me" but untested cold, logged-out, on a different network — verify as the literal last pre-submission step (G4).
- [ ] **Cliff/threshold logic:** Often smoothly interpolated in early prototypes — verify explicit unit tests exist at every named threshold boundary (A1.6).

## Recovery Strategies

| Pitfall | Recovery Cost | Recovery Steps |
|---------|-----------------|------------------|
| A curated jurisdiction's number doesn't match a validation pair, discovered late | MEDIUM | Apply the B5 mismatch taxonomy immediately: check for a known explainable factor (uplift, allocation-stage, season aggregation) before assuming a bug; if genuinely a bug, it's cheaper to fix the specific rule than to abandon the jurisdiction — but if time doesn't permit a fix, demote that specific pair from the demo script rather than showing a broken match |
| Job 2's live-research chain proves too unreliable for a live demo close to the deadline | LOW-MEDIUM | Fall back to H3's plan: keep the hosted app genuinely live (Stage One requirement), but use the best of several pre-recorded genuinely-live takes for the video, and add explicit informative loading/error states for direct judge interaction |
| Balances (the public index) is clearly not going to be finished in full | LOW | Ship H4's minimum publishable version — a static/manual reference-production run, one real change-log entry, and the republished Job 1 accuracy figure; drop reverse mode entirely, it's already flagged as the correct first cut |
| CT's CSV schema issue proves harder to resolve than expected | LOW | Demote CT from curated to live-researched (H1's stated fallback); NY/CA/NJ alone still clear the three-validated-award Definition of Done with room to spare |
| A GitHub license/SDK/hosting Stage One check fails close to the deadline | LOW, if caught early / HIGH, if caught after submission closes | Run the full G1-G4 checklist at least 24 hours before the deadline, not at the deadline, specifically to leave recovery time |
